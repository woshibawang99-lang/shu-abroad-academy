When optimizing the dynamic local loss function, every node tracks the time-varying global optimal solution by communicating with neighboring nodes via communication networks in a cooperative way. We propose a novel online distributed Push–Pull algorithm and present that the proposed online optimization algorithm can track the dynamic optimal solution with proper step sizes. We analyze the dynamic regret of the proposed algorithm in two cases where the global loss function is strongly convex and smooth, or is convex, smooth and Lipschitz. Our results illustrate that the dynamic regret of the proposed online optimization algorithm can be sublinear, if the path length and the gradient variance are sublinear. At last, we demonstrate the property of the online distributed optimization algorithm by two simulation examples. 2025 Elsevier Ltd. Introduction Nowadays, online distributed convex optimization problems have obtained plenty of research attention in machine learning, large-scale statistical tasks, sensor networks, and so on, such as Fosson ( 2020 ). For example, with the rapid growth of the number of images in image classification tasks, online distributed algorithms can be used to learn and track the computer’s ca- pability of classifying image in a timely manner, which is an effective tool to guarantee the accuracy of image classification after the computer learn massive quantity of images ( Li, Xie, & Li , 2023 ). Moreover, with the increasing demand of tracking moving targets, a helpful method to solve this problem is to use multiple sensors to track the target in a cooperative way ( Li et al. , 2023 ). How to track the moving targets timely and effectively via the time-varying information collected by sensors is a key issue which needs to be solved. Since tracking moving targets in nature may be interrupted at any time, online distributed algorithms can be utilized to track moving targets and re-plan routes when en- countering disturbances. In general, online optimization algorithms can be classified into two categories, i.e., online centralized ones and online distributed ones. Compared with the online centralized algo- rithms ( Hazan, Agarwal, & Kale , 2007 ; Yi et al. , 2021 ; Zinkevich , August, 2003 ), distributed ones do not rely on a fusion center (i.e., receive every agent’s information using a central unit and make a global estimation of the dynamic optimal solution), but via a network topology, such that neighboring nodes can estimate the optimal solution cooperatively by communications. In this manner, even if some nodes do not work, the estimation process will not be influenced through communications. Also, when the global loss function changes with time, dynamic regret is a useful metric that can be used to reflect the performances of online optimization algorithms. Therefore, to conduct research on the dynamic regret analysis of online distributed algorithms is of great significance. To solve the online distributed optimization problems, researchers from all over the world have conducted a series of research works. For examples, Yi, Li, Xie, and Johansson ( 2020 ) proposed an online distributed primal–dual dynamic mirror descent algorithm with a strongly connected and balanced communication network, and demonstrated that the upper bound of the static regret is sublinear for proper step sizes.
